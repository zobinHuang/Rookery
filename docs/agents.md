# Agents

The sidebar's Agents mode shows every code agent roost knows of, one crow each playing the state its session is in, and the content area shows one agent's conversation with a box to prompt it. What an agent is, and the events that move it between states, are in [resources](resources.md); how a prompt reaches its pane is in [rook's live link](../rook/docs/link.md).

## Following roost

`FleetModel` (one for the app) holds the fleet. With an address in Settings and the token in a file only the user may read (`FileSecret`, `roost-token` in the data directory, moved once from the keychain, where an unanswered prompt after a rebuild had left the app unconnected; a failed read is tried again on the next connect), it reads the agents and rooks over HTTP, then opens roost's live link as a desktop and applies each event to `AgentFleet` (`RookeryCore`): a state or an end changes the agent in place, a new agent is fetched. When the link drops it shows the last fleet with a banner and retries, backing off from 2 to 60 seconds, then reads everything afresh, so nothing between the drop and the reconnect is missed.

`RoostClient` (`RookeryPlatform`) is the only code that speaks roost's API.

## The sidebar

- Sessions only; a session's subagents fold under it behind a count.
- The user's teams first, then the rest by last activity, then those that ended, folded away.
- Teams live on roost ([teams](../rook/docs/teams.md)), so their agents know them too; an agent may be in several, and shows under each, tagged with the others. This Mac keeps only the layout in `agent-teams.json`: the order of the teams and of the agents in each, and which are folded. Teams made here before roost held them are carried over to it once. Drag an agent onto a team to add it, holding Option to move it out of the team it was dragged from; onto an agent in a team to put it before; onto New Team to start one; or onto Unassigned to take it out of the team it came from. Drag a team's header to reorder teams. A change shows at once and is written to roost; if roost refuses it, the sidebar says why and takes roost's teams back.
- Each agent's row shows what it works on now, or its role, as Rookery keeps them or the agent set them with `rook me`.
- A machine whose rook is offline dims its agents.

## The crows

`branding/` renders each state's loop into `Resources/Crow_<state>.png` (a strip of 64-pixel frames) and `Resources/Crows.json`. `CrowView` plays them through `PixelAnimationView`, the same layer the sidebar logo uses: a discrete keyframe animation on the layer's contents, scaled without smoothing, so the loops cost the app nothing while they play and stand still under Reduce Motion. At 32 points on a Retina screen a crow is drawn pixel for pixel.

## The agent's page

`AgentDetailView` reads the session's transcript from roost every two seconds, only the bytes it has not seen, through `TranscriptReader` (`RookeryCore`), which understands Claude Code transcripts and codex rollouts and keeps what a person reads: the prompts, the answers and one line per tool call. Prompts typed in its box go to `POST /v1/messages`; under the box, the latest prompts show what became of them (waiting while the agent is busy, delivered, or failed and why).

## Checking it without a person

Seed a roost of your own with agents (`POST /v1/events` with `started` and `state` events, a transcript through the session upload API) and render `StageView` offscreen with a `FleetModel` given an in-memory `SecretStore`, so the render never touches the token file; see docs/development.md for the harness.

## Rookmaster

The console over everything roost manages, opened from the top of the Agents sidebar. `FleetModel` keeps a `ResourceGraph` (`RookeryCore`) beside the fleet: every kind roost lists, all their resources, the relations between them and the latest hundred events, read in full on connecting and kept current by the same live link.

- **Board** (`RookmasterBoard`): a column per kind, agents leftmost, each ordered by last use so what moved last rises, with an animation. Edges are the relations, drawn from the cards' frames (anchor preferences), thicker for relations that happened more often, coloured by verb; a subagent's edges are drawn from its session's card. Hovering or clicking a card brings forward it, its neighbours and the edges between them; double-clicking opens it. Lock freezes the order; the filters narrow by name, by time since last use, to live agents, and by kind.
- **A list per kind** (`RookmasterKindList`), with an editor beside it (`ResourceEditors.swift`):
  - skills are added by pasting their address (a GitHub or GitLab page of the skill's directory or its SKILL.md, or `owner/repo@path#ref`); the editor shows where the skill lives and the commit roost resolved, and changes its name, description and tracked ref, refreshes it, or removes it;
  - playbooks are created, edited and deleted: what they are for, their steps, and the skills they load. A step names a skill it uses with `@`; saving pins every skill the steps name (`PlaybookSteps` in `RookeryCore`), beside those added by hand;
  - memories are created, edited and deleted: their name, project and text;
  - agents are read-only, and any other kind is edited as a name and an attributes object.
- **Log** (`RookmasterLog`): the latest hundred events, newest at the bottom, following as they are logged, with a filter; it folds to its title bar.

## Hosts and new agents

The Hosts tab of Rookmaster lists the concrete hosts of `~/.ssh/config` (patterns and `Match` blocks name none; `Include` is followed), each with the rook it runs: the one the user chose, else a rook named as the host or its short host name. A host's page checks over ssh what it has for agents (tmux, claude, codex, rook, and whether rook reports to a roost), installs rook where it has none (after asking: it runs rook's `setup.sh --name` and `rook connect` there, the token going over ssh's input, never a command line), and keeps the host's workdirs: a directory, browsable over ssh, with the harness, playbook, skills, role and teams an agent started there begins with. Hosts' settings and workdirs live in `hosts.json` in the app's data directory, since only this Mac's ssh configuration reaches the hosts. ssh runs with `BatchMode`, so a host that asks for a password fails rather than waiting.

New Agent, in the Agents list, on a host's page and on each workdir, starts an agent through roost rather than ssh: a spawn with a launch id ([the API](../server/API.md#messages)), which the host's rook-link starts in tmux after installing the playbook and skills; the session names its launch as it starts, and roost gives it its role and teams. Until it reports in, the launch shows as starting under the button; a rook that could not start it, or a session that never reports in within 90 seconds, shows why, naming the pane it was started in: a session in a folder Claude Code has not trusted waits there for a person to answer its trust prompt.

## Teams

The Teams tab of Rookmaster shows each team: its goal, which its agents are told; its members' cards with their role, focus, state and host, and their duty in the team, which the user edits in place; the messages its agents sent one another and to the whole team, oldest first; a box to speak to the whole team as the user; and the limits past which roost holds a message. A held message shows its reason with Cancel and Let It Go; the tab and the team's row count those still held. An agent's page shows its role, which the user may change, its focus and its teams.

Rookery keeps every rook agent's role (who it is to its teammates) and focus (what it works on now) written, so no one has to (`AgentIdentityKeeper`). Every 90 seconds it reads what each live agent's transcript grew by on roost, through a `TranscriptReader` begun where it last stopped, and digests it (`TranscriptDigest`: how many prompts and compactions, the first and latest prompts, the latest compaction summary and reply). An agent's identity is due once the user first prompts it and it lacks a role or a focus, and again after its context is compacted or 20 prompts later; Rookery then asks the code agent Raven runs on, with its smallest model, no tools and no session kept (`claude -p --model haiku --tools "" --no-session-persistence`, or `codex exec --ephemeral`), for one JSON line of each, and writes them with `role_source` and `focus_source` set to `auto` and `identity_checked_offset` set to the transcript's length. Where each came from decides what it may overwrite: a role the user wrote on the agent's page (`user`) or the agent wrote with `rook me role` (`agent`) is kept as written; one a launch gave (`launch`, which Raven always writes when it proposes an agent) is the starting point and is brought up to date like Rookery's own; a focus the agent set itself stands for an hour. Emptying the role on the agent's page hands it back to Rookery. A transcript first seen, or grown by more than 8 MB since, is read only at its last 2 MB, and its start for the first prompt; an agent the model failed for waits ten minutes.

## Raven

The Rookmaster console's panel under the board, which ⌘J folds away and opens again on Raven, asks Raven, Rookery's agent over the fleet, for something in plain words; `@` names a resource, completed from the graph (`Mentions` in `RookeryCore`), and the request carries each named resource's kind, name and id. `RavenAgent` runs Claude Code headless (`HeadlessAgent` in `RookeryPlatform`) in a workspace of its own under the data directory, laid out afresh for each request from the app's `Raven` resources (`Resources/Raven` in the repository):

- `CLAUDE.md`, its standing instructions, and `.claude/skills/`, Rookery's skills: `rookery-resources` (the command reference), `fleet-status`, `inspect-agent` and `triage-attention` (the agents' state, what one is doing, what waits on the user, through `rook-client agent status` and `agent tail`), `brief-agent` (a message or an interrupt for an agent, proposed), `start-agent` (a new agent on a host or workdir, with its playbook, skills, role and teams, proposed), `manage-teams` (a new team, a member and its duty, proposed), `direct-team` (directing a team the conversation is in), `team-report` (where a team stands and what blocks it), `register-skill`, `write-playbook`, `distill-playbook`, `tidy-memories`, `describe-skills` and `review-fleet`.
- `bin/rook-client`, the same client rook installs, and `config/rook/connect.json`, the app's roost address and token, readable by the user alone.
- `config/rook/hosts.json`, the user's ssh hosts with their rooks and workdirs, which `rook-client host list` reads: they live on this Mac, not on roost.

The box closes to one line once a request is sent, keeping the focus, and opens again with the next key; the conversation follows its end as the answer comes in unless the user scrolled up, Rookery's own scrolling never counting as theirs. A conversation is titled by its first request until Raven first answers; then Rookery writes a title of a few words from the request and the answer with the same small model (`QuickModel`), since `claude -p` writes none (`RavenConversation.titleSource`: none, `generated` or `user`, which a rename sets and no title written replaces), keeps it with the conversation and tells roost with a `renamed` event; the latest twenty conversations kept from before are titled when Raven first loads. The title heads the open conversation, the panel's list and the launcher's, and names the conversation in the log, as `Raven [title]`, since every one of them goes by the same name. A conversation runs on Claude Code or on Codex, chosen in the panel before it starts and kept for its turns (`RavenConversation.engine`). Codex runs as `codex exec --json` (`exec resume <thread>` for later turns) with none of the user's configuration, commands sandboxed to the workspace with the network open for rook-client, nothing asked of anyone, and `rookery-mail` as its MCP server; it reads `AGENTS.md` and `.agents/skills`, copies of `CLAUDE.md` and the skills. Its events are translated into Claude Code's transcript form (`CodexTranslation`), so one reader shows both and proposals are found the same way. Its sandbox reads the whole Mac, where Claude Code's allow list keeps Raven to rook-client.

Both get the user's mail and calendars from `rookery-mail` ([mail and calendars](mail.md)).

Claude Code runs with `--setting-sources project`, so neither the user's settings nor their hooks apply, and `--allowedTools "Bash(rook-client resource:*)" "Bash(rook-client agent:*)" "Bash(rook-client propose:*)" "Bash(rook-client host:*)" "Bash(rook-client team list:*)" "Bash(rook-client team roster:*)" "Bash(rook-client team send:*)" "Bash(rook-client message:*)" "Bash(rook-client inbox:*)" Skill WebFetch`: it reads and changes roost's resources, reads the agents' state and transcripts, loads its skills and reads a skill's own pages, and messages agents only as a member of a team (below), which roost enforces. What it would do to an agent or on a rook it proposes with `rook-client propose`: `message`, `interrupt`, `approve` (allow the waiting call once, or decline it) or `spawn` (a new session in a directory on a rook); each checks its agent or rook and prints the proposal without doing anything. The app finds each proposal in Raven's output and history (`RavenProposal` in `RookeryCore`) and shows it under the conversation with a button that carries it out and Not Now; carrying it out posts it to roost as the user, as a delivery of its kind for the rook to perform (rook/docs/link.md), and follows the delivery. Each decision is kept with the conversation (`RavenDecision`) and told to Raven at the start of its next request.

To check the plumbing without calling a model, set `executableOverride` to a script that prints stream-json lines and runs `rook-client` itself, and `workspaceOverride` to a scratch directory.

### Raven in a team

The user puts a conversation in a team from its header (Join a Team, once it has begun), and takes it out from its chip or from the team's page, where it shows as Raven with a way to the conversation; the sidebar leaves it out of its teams. roost holds it to that team as any member (server/API.md#messages): it messages the team's members itself, with `rook-client message` and `rook-client team send`, named to rook-client by `ROOK_AGENT_ID` since it runs outside tmux, and reaches no one else. Its teammates read it as Raven, the user's assistant speaking for the user, in their rosters and in its messages (rook/docs/teams.md). Each request lists the conversation's teams and members. Their answers wait on roost under the rook `rookery`; every four seconds while any conversation is in a team, `RavenAgent` reads them, reports each delivered, keeps each in its conversation in rook's own words (`RookMessage`), which the conversation shows as the sender's, not the user's, and wakes the conversation to answer them, or once it finishes the answer under way.
